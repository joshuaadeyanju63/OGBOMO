Why Ogbomosoland needs this

Ogbomosoland's waste problem is not a mystery — it is documented. This platform is built to close a gap researchers have already identified as logistical, not technological.

~745K Residents across Ogbomosoland as of 2024 — up from roughly 166,000 in 1991 and 25,000 in 1885. Growth researchers have linked directly to worsening waste management strain.
>50% Share of Ogbomoso's household waste found to be organic material in a local waste-characterization study — meaning delayed collection actively decomposes, smells, and blocks drains.
5 + 7 Five local governments plus seven local council development areas (LCDAs) — twelve local administrative units across Ogbomosoland. A shared reporting and dispatch view across them is what Ogbomọ́ proposes. What does this mean?

Reading the numbers

What does “5 + 7 = 12” mean?

Ogbomosoland is administered by five Local Government Areas (LGAs): Ogbomosho North, Ogbomosho South, Oriire, Ogo-Oluwa and Surulere. On top of those, Oyo State created seven Local Council Development Areas (LCDAs) in 2016, smaller administrative units meant to bring government closer to communities. Five LGAs plus seven LCDAs gives twelve local administrative units, each with its own office and area of responsibility.

Why this matters for waste: a resident needs to know which office a problem belongs to, and an officer needs to see only the reports for their area. Ogbomọ́ starts by routing every report to one of the five LGAs. Adding the LCDAs is a possible later step.

Pilot goals — not yet results

What success looks like

  • Cut average time-to-resolution on resident reports to under 24 hours
  • Give all five local governments one shared, real-time view of collection status
  • Turn resident photo reports into a verifiable evidence trail the council can act on
  • Produce the route and volume data needed to plan staffing and truck allocation with evidence, not guesswork
  • Establish at least one working connection between sorted household recyclables and a paying buyer
Sources

Peer-reviewed studies on Ogbomoso solid waste generation and composition (2015–2021); Ogbomosho South local government waste-logistics research; national solid waste statistics from Nigerian waste policy literature; Ogbomosho population data (Wikipedia, 2024 estimate).
